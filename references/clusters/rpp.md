# Method inventory — Remarks on the Philosophy of Psychology (Vol. I)

The source for aspect-seeing, the grammar of the inner, and psychological concepts as networks. Where
PI states these moves, RPP *works* them — the volume opens on aspect and returns to it for a thousand
remarks, trying case after case. Section numbers are from Anscombe's edition of TS 229 (RPP I,
§§1–1137).

**Measured register (firsthand English text, 78,298 words).** The most interrogative setting in the
corpus — 27% of sentences end in a question, the highest anywhere — and the shortest remarks: 23% of
sentences run to eight words or fewer, median 16. Hedge:booster 2.38. 27% of sentences carry a quoted
voice; the interlocutor is present almost continuously. Dashes are heavy (11.5 per 1,000). This is the
*patient investigation* setting: try a case, ask what it shows, try another. Full baseline in
`../voice.md`.

**A citation correction worth noting:** earlier builds of this file located the aspect material at
"§§478 ff." That is the Moore's-Paradox and believing sequence. The aspect work is at §§1–70 (where the
volume begins), §§411–413, §§508–520 and §§858–871. Citations below are the corrected ones.

## Moves

### 1. Aspect-switch — seeing-as is not seeing plus interpreting
- **Question it lets you ask:** is this a dispute about the facts, or about which *aspect* of the same
  thing each party is taking? What does one aspect reveal that the other hides?
- **From the book:** the volume opens on exactly this. "Is it introspection that tells me whether I have
  to do with a genuine seeing, or rather with an act of interpreting?" (§2). "The expression of the
  experience is: 'Now I'm seeing it as a pyramid; now as a square with its diagonals.' Now, what is the
  'it' which I see now this way, now that?" (§31). And the resolution of the seeing/interpreting
  dilemma at §20: "an interpretation becomes an expression of experience. And the interpretation is not
  an indirect description; no, it is the primary expression of the experience."
- **Serves:** Mode 2's Engine A (generating alternative framings); Mode 1's Sharpening 1.
- **Fails when:** "it's just a matter of perspective" is smuggled in under the name. An aspect is not
  an opinion. §518: "No aspect that is not (also) conception" — the aspect carries a way of taking the
  thing, and different ways of taking it have different consequences, which is exactly why the move is
  worth making.

### 2. The dawning of an aspect vs. continuous seeing
- **Question:** is the person *reporting a steady perception* or *registering a change in how it
  strikes them*? These are two uses of "see", and confusing them breeds pseudo-problems.
- **From the book:** "If someone notices for the first time that the picture is ambiguous, he might
  react, say with the exclamation: 'Ah! a rabbit!'; but still, when he now goes on seeing the picture
  continuously in one aspect, he won't want to keep on exclaiming" (§861). "I want to say that the
  natural, primitive expression of the experience of an aspect would be such an exclamation; it might
  also be a lighting up of the eyes. (Something strikes me!)" (§862). And §864: continuous seeing means
  "this description, without any variation, is the right one — and *that* only means that the aspect
  did not change."
- **Serves:** Mode 1's distinction between report and expression; Mode 2's naming of a stuck dispute as
  one party's aspect having dawned and the other's not.
- **The transportable form:** when someone says "I finally *see* what you mean," they are not reporting
  new information. They are reporting that a description has become the natural one. Do not treat that
  as a factual claim needing evidence, and do not treat it as merely psychological either.

### 3. The genealogical tree — a concept-network, not one essence
- **Question:** instead of "what *is* belief / expectation / intention?", ask how this concept sits
  among its neighbours — what it borrows, what it is contrasted with, where the family branches.
- **From the book:** "I should like to speak of a genealogical tree of psychological concepts. (Is there
  here a similarity to a genealogical tree of different number concepts?)" (§722). "Anxiety borrows the
  pictures of fear. 'I have the feeling of impending doom'" (§724). And the aim, stated flatly at §895:
  "The genealogical tree of psychological phenomena: I strive, not for exactness, but for a view of the
  whole."
- **Serves:** Mode 2's Engine B (family resemblance for forced either/ors); Mode 1's resistance to the
  demand for one essence behind a psychological word.
- **Why §895 matters beyond psychology:** it is the corpus's clearest statement of what a good answer
  aims at. Not a definition that survives every edge case — a *view of the whole* in which the edge
  cases can be placed. That is the same deliverable PI §122 calls a surveyable representation.

### 4. The grammar of the inner is fixed by its surroundings
- **Question:** what in the *setting* — the circumstances, what came before, what the person goes on to
  do — makes this the expecting / intending / meaning it is? Not: what private episode accompanies it?
- **From the book:** "When one learns to use the word 'pain', that does not happen through guessing
  which of the inner processes connected with falling down etc. this word is used for" (§305). "In order
  to doubt whether the other is feeling pain now, I need the concept of pain; not pain" (§154) — the
  concept is not distilled from the sensation. And §287, putting the objection at its strongest: "So
  does psychology deal with behaviour (*say*), not with human states of mind? If someone does a
  psychological experiment — what will he report? — What the subject says, what he does, what has
  happened to him in the past and how he has reacted to it."
- **Serves:** the core's "no inner object where the grammar calls for a criterion" refusal; Mode 2
  whenever a team posits a hidden "intent", "understanding", or "preference" that no procedure could
  check.
- **Fails when:** read as denying inner life. §287 is a question he is pressing, not a thesis he holds.

### 5. Uncertainty about another person is not a defect in our evidence
- **Question:** is this uncertainty a gap that better instruments would close, or is it constitutive of
  the concept?
- **From the book:** on whether someone is pretending — "of course it is also uncertain whether he is not
  merely putting up an appearance of pretending… But what does this uncertainty consist in? Am I really
  always in some uncertainty whether someone is really angry, sad, glad etc. etc.? No" — no more than
  whether there is a notebook in front of me (§137). And the meaning-blind man who "would not feel that
  the names, when heard or seen, were distinguished by an imponderable Something" (§243).
- **Serves:** Mode 2 in any dispute about measuring something human — engagement, intent, satisfaction,
  understanding. The characteristic finding is not "we need a better metric" but "the concept was never
  the kind that a metric closes."

### 6. "What is the content of…?" is often a wrongly-framed question
- **Question:** is the demand for the *content* of a state a real question, or a picture pretending to
  be one?
- **From the book:** "But what is the content, the content of consciousness, in anxiety? The question is
  wrongly framed" (§725). "'A picture (mental image, memory image) of longing.' One thinks that one has
  already done everything by speaking of a 'picture'" (§726).
- **Serves:** Mode 1's explanatory-mirage detection; Mode 3's translation of "content of experience" talk
  back into what a person notices, says, and does.
- **The generalization:** "one thinks one has already done everything by speaking of a picture" applies
  far past psychology. Whenever an explanation terminates in a noun that names the thing it was supposed
  to explain — *culture*, *alignment*, *intent*, *the model's understanding* — the same diagnosis
  applies.

### 7. Not to explain, but to accept the phenomenon
- **Question:** am I looking for a mechanism under this, when what is called for is to let it stand and
  describe its surroundings?
- **From the book:** "*Not* to explain, but to *accept* the psychological phenomenon — that is what is
  difficult" (§509).
- **Serves:** the core's first refusal — describe where you might explain — stated here more plainly than
  anywhere else in the corpus, and with the word that matters: *difficult*. The refusal costs something,
  and he says so.

### 8. Our concepts, not the child's — analysis of concepts is not analysis of what happens
- **Question:** is this an investigation of a process, or of the words we use about it?
- **From the book:** "what is in question here is not an analysis of what happens. Only an analysis —
  and this word is very misleading — of our concepts. And our concepts are more complicated than those
  of the child; in so far, that is, as our words have a more complicated employment than its words do"
  (§413). Setting it up, §412: "Would a child understand what it means to see the table 'as a table'? It
  learns: 'This is a table, that's a bench'… without any hint of there being an aspect involved in the
  business."
- **Serves:** Mode 1's separation of the conceptual question from the empirical one; Mode 2's Step 0.
  When a team is running an experiment to settle what a word should mean, this is the move.

### 9. What strikes you as queer while philosophizing is not queer
- **Question:** is this strangeness in the phenomenon, or in the prototype I have silently adopted for
  how the word must work?
- **From the book:** "what strikes someone as queer when he is philosophizing is not queer. We make the
  assumption: the word… would really have to be used like *this* (*this* use strikes us as a prototype)
  and then we find the normal use extremely queer" (§1074).
- **Serves:** Mode 1's pseudo-depth detection; the core's refusal to honour the feeling of depth as
  evidence. This is that refusal's mechanism, named: the sense of strangeness comes from the prototype
  you brought, not from the thing you are looking at.

## The voice of this book

The shortest remarks in the corpus and the highest question rate. Many remarks are a single question
with no answer attached; some are a single sentence that only makes sense as the next step in a run of
twenty. The dash carries the turn. He addresses himself constantly — "I should like to say", "I want to
say", "Am I now to say…?" — and then declines his own formulation. Two moves are especially worth
imitating: the *bare exclamation of a case* ("Ah! a rabbit!") and the *one-line remark that refuses the
whole preceding run* ("Not to explain, but to accept").

## How this cluster changes the base skill
The base skill treated the inner mostly as avowal-versus-report. RPP adds three things. First, *aspect*
as a third category between fact and interpretation, which the inspector needs for figurative and
framing statements. Second, *concept-networks* (§722, §895), which give Mode 2 its family-resemblance
engine for psychological and evaluative terms and name the deliverable — a view of the whole rather than
a definition. Third, §509 and §1074, which supply the *mechanism* behind two of the core's refusals:
accepting a phenomenon is hard, and the feeling of strangeness comes from an imported prototype. Load
this file whenever the problem involves perception, framing, mental-state vocabulary, or any attempt to
measure something whose concept may not admit of measurement.
