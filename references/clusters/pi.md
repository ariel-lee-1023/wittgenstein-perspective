# Method inventory — Philosophical Investigations (en face German–English, 4th ed.)

PI is this skill's home text: use, language-game, criterion, grammar, picture, family resemblance,
rule-following. This file holds the moves *in working form* — the question each one lets you ask, the
located evidence, what it serves, and how it fails when it is used badly — plus the places the
**German original sharpens or shifts a key term**, which is why the en-face edition is the corpus
copy.

Two numbering systems are in play. A plain `§` is Part I. `PPF §` is the fourth edition's *Philosophy
of Psychology — A Fragment*, previously printed as Part II; that renumbering is one of the practical
reasons to work from this edition rather than citing "Part II, §xi".

**Measured register (firsthand English text, 95,674 words).** The longest remarks in the corpus —
sentence mean 24.1, median 19, p90 46, and 15% of sentences running past forty words, the highest
anywhere here. 28% of sentences carry a quoted voice, joint highest with OC. Hedge:booster 2.57, the
middle of the range. This is the *patient* setting: the objection gets room, and the counter-move
takes its time. Full baseline in `../voice.md`.

## Moves

### 1. Name the picture that holds us captive
- **Question it lets you ask:** which *image* is silently steering this problem — and would the
  problem survive putting it down?
- **From the book:** "A *picture* held us captive. And we couldn't get outside it, for it lay in our
  language, and language seemed only to repeat it to us inexorably" (§115). The remedy comes in the
  next remark: "What *we* do is to bring words back from their metaphysical to their everyday use"
  (§116).
- **Serves:** core move 2; Mode 2's "picture in charge" step; the core's refusal to honour the feeling
  of depth.
- **Fails when:** you name a picture the other party never had. The test is whether *they* recognise
  it as what they had been assuming. If not, you have supplied a straw image; drop it.

### 2. Meaning is use — and "use" means an employment in a practice
- **Question:** in what circumstances would someone say this, what would they do with it, what
  response would show they had understood?
- **From the book:** "the meaning of a word is its use in the language" (§43) — stated, note, for a
  large class of cases and not as a definition. And §11: "Think of the tools in a toolbox: there is a
  hammer, pliers, a saw, a screwdriver, a rule, a glue-pot, glue, nails and screws. — The functions of
  words are as diverse as the functions of these objects."
- **Serves:** core move 1 and core move 10 (bring the word home).
- **Fails when:** it hardens into a theory of meaning. §43 is a reminder about where to look, not a
  doctrine to defend. If you find yourself arguing *for* "meaning is use", you have left the method.

### 3. Language-games — language woven into activity
- **Question:** what activity is this sentence part of — reporting, ordering, avowing, promising,
  classifying, founding a method, protesting? And is that activity's own standard of correctness in
  view?
- **From the book:** "There are *countless* kinds; countless different kinds of use of all the things
  we call 'signs', 'words', 'sentences'. And this diversity is not something fixed, given once for
  all; but new types of language, new language-games, as we may say, come into existence, and others
  become obsolete and get forgotten" (§23). In the same remark: "the *speaking* of language is part of
  an activity, or of a form of life."
- **Serves:** Mode 2's "which game are we playing?"; Mode 1's locate-the-game step.
- **Fails when:** "language-game" gets used as a label for a subject area ("the language-game of
  science"). A game is a *practised activity with its own moves*. If you cannot say what someone does
  in it and what would count as a wrong move, you have not identified one.

### 4. Family resemblance — overlapping strands, no shared essence
- **Question:** do these cases share one essence, or are they a family joined by criss-crossing
  similarities? Is a demanded either/or drawing a boundary the practice never drew?
- **From the book:** on games — "look and see whether there is anything common to all… don't think,
  but look!"; the upshot is "a complicated network of similarities overlapping and criss-crossing"
  (§66). Then §67: "I can think of no better expression to characterize these similarities than
  'family resemblances'"; and the thread — "the strength of the thread resides not in the fact that
  some one fibre runs through its whole length, but in the overlapping of many fibres."
- **The counterweight, which matters as much as the move:** §71 — "One can say that the concept of a
  game is a concept with blurred edges. — 'But is a blurred concept a *concept* at all?' — Is a
  photograph that is not sharp a picture of a person at all?" And §88 on drawing a boundary for a
  purpose: "is it senseless to say 'Stay roughly here'?"
- **Serves:** Mode 2's Engine B; Mode 1's resistance to essence-hunting.
- **Fails when:** it becomes a licence for vagueness. The move is never "the question has no answer".
  It is "the answer is *drawn*, and drawn for something — so say what for."

### 5. Rule-following — grasping a rule that is not an interpretation
- **Question:** does the rule fix its own applications, or does the practice? What would count as "the
  same" here — and where does the rule run out?
- **From the book:** "no course of action could be determined by a rule, because every course of
  action can be made out to accord with the rule" (§201) — resolved in §202: "That's why 'following a
  rule' is a practice. And to *think* one is following a rule is not to follow a rule." Where the
  demand for justification stops: "Once I have exhausted the justifications, I have reached bedrock,
  and my spade is turned. Then I am inclined to say: 'This is simply what I do'" (§217). And §211: "If
  that means 'Have I reasons?', the answer is: my reasons will soon give out. And then I shall act,
  without reasons."
- **Serves:** Mode 2's Engine C; Mode 1's Sharpening 2; the core's refusal to hand over a foundation.
- **Fails when:** read as scepticism about rules. Nothing here says a rule cannot be followed
  correctly. It says correctness is fixed by a trained practice rather than by the words alone.

### 6. Philosophy describes; it leaves everything as it is
- **Question:** am I being asked to *reform* the language, or to *describe* it?
- **From the book:** "we may not advance any kind of theory… all *explanation* must go, and
  description alone must take its place" (§109), where philosophy is "a struggle against the
  bewitchment of our understanding by the resources of our language". Then §124: "It leaves everything
  as it is." §127: "The work of the philosopher consists in marshalling recollections for a particular
  purpose." And the target condition, §133: "The real discovery is the one that enables me to break
  off philosophizing when I want to. — The one that gives philosophy peace"; and in the same remark,
  "There is not a *single* philosophical method, though there are indeed methods, different therapies,
  as it were."
- **Serves:** the core's anti-system and anti-explanation refusals; the whole descriptive stance.
- **Fails when:** taken for quietism. Describing is work — it takes assembling the cases, and it ends
  with the problem gone, not with a shrug.

### 7. Nothing is hidden — the difficulty is one of *survey*, not of depth
- **Question:** is what we lack a further fact, or an overview of what is already in plain sight?
- **From the book:** "We want to *understand* something that is already in plain view" (§89). "The
  aspects of things that are most important for us are hidden because of their simplicity and
  familiarity. (One is unable to notice something — because it is always before one's eyes.)" (§129).
  And the methodological demand: "our grammar is deficient in surveyability. A surveyable
  representation produces precisely that kind of understanding which consists in 'seeing connections'"
  (§122).
- **Serves:** Mode 1's pseudo-depth detection; Mode 2's move from "we need more data" to "we need the
  cases laid side by side". *Übersichtliche Darstellung* — a surveyable representation — is the
  closest thing in the book to a positive deliverable, and it is what a good Mode 2 answer actually
  produces: the cases arranged so the connections show.
- **Fails when:** used to wave away real empirical ignorance. Mode 2's Step 0 exists for exactly this.

### 8. A philosophical problem has the form "I don't know my way about"
- **Question:** what *kind* of stuckness is this — a missing fact, or a lost orientation?
- **From the book:** "A philosophical problem has the form: 'I don't know my way about'" (§123). And
  §107 on the pull toward the ideal: "we are unable to walk. We want to walk: so we need *friction*.
  Back to the rough ground!"
- **Serves:** Mode 2's triage; the core's preference for the particular case over the clean system.
- **Fails when:** every disorientation is read as philosophical. Sometimes a person is lost because
  they lack the map, not because the map is confused.

### 9. The inner needs outward criteria; intention is embedded in a setting
- **Question:** what in the surrounding customs and institutions makes this the intending / meaning /
  expecting it is? (Not: what private episode accompanies it?)
- **From the book:** "An 'inner process' stands in need of outward criteria" (§580). "An intention is
  embedded in a setting, in human customs and institutions. If the technique of the game of chess did
  not exist, I could not intend to play a game of chess" (§337). The beetle-in-a-box conclusion at
  §293: "one can 'divide through' by the thing in the box; it cancels out, whatever it is" — the
  private object drops out of the account, not because it does not exist but because it does no work
  there. And §271: "a wheel that can be turned though nothing else moves with it is not part of the
  mechanism."
- **Serves:** the core's refusal to posit an inner object where the grammar calls for a criterion;
  Mode 2 whenever a team posits a hidden "intent", "understanding", or "preference" no procedure could
  check.
- **Fails when:** turned into behaviourism. §304 is explicit: "It's not a Something, but not a Nothing
  either!" The target is the object-and-name grammar, not the inner life.

### 10. A sign is dead; use gives it life
- **Question:** is this expression doing anything, or is it being held up and admired?
- **From the book:** "Every sign *by itself* seems dead. *What* gives it life? — In use it *lives*"
  (§432).
- **Serves:** Mode 1's central operation; Mode 3's translation test — if the re-voiced sentence loses
  all its force on the way down into practice, that is the finding, and it gets reported plainly.

### 11. Agreement is in form of life, not in opinions
- **Question:** what has to be shared already for this dispute to be *possible*?
- **From the book:** "This is agreement not in opinions, but rather in form of life" (§241); and §242:
  "It is not only agreement in definitions, but also (odd as it may sound) agreement in judgements that
  is required for communication." What terminates the regress, PPF §345: "What has to be accepted, the
  given, is — one might say — *forms of life*."
- **Serves:** Mode 2's "which game?"; the core's account of where justification stops. Pairs directly
  with OC §110 — PI names the shared background, OC names the acting.

### 12. Aspect — seeing-as is not seeing plus interpreting
- **Question:** is this a dispute about the facts, or about which aspect of the same thing each party
  is taking?
- **From the book:** PPF §118: "the following figure, derived from Jastrow, will be called 'the
  duck–rabbit'. It can be seen as a rabbit's head or as a duck's. And I must distinguish between the
  'continuous seeing' of an aspect and an aspect's 'lighting up'." PPF §235: "'The echo of a thought in
  sight' — one would like to say." *Aspect-blindness* is introduced as a conceptual investigation, not
  a psychological hypothesis.
- **Serves:** Mode 2's Engine A; Mode 1's Sharpening 1. Worked out at length in `rpp.md` — load that
  file when aspect is the centre of the problem rather than a step in it.

## Where the German sharpens the sense (why en-face matters)

- **Satz.** No single English equivalent — it spans *sentence*, *proposition*, and even *remark*, and
  the translators choose case by case. *Effect on method:* do not hang an argument on the English word
  "proposition" as if it named one thing; ask which use of *Satz* is in play — a sentence-form, a claim
  with a truth-value, or a remark. The looseness of *Satz* is itself an argument against levelling
  propositions (core move 5). Cf. §§134–137 on "This is how things are" as the general form of *Satz*.
- **Bild.** *Picture / image / model* — wider than the English "picture". In §115 (*Ein Bild hielt uns
  gefangen*) it is a captivating mental image; in RFM a proof is a *Bild*, a model. *Effect:* "picture"
  in this skill always carries the Bild-range — a steering image *and* a working model — never a mere
  illustration. It is also the corpus's most frequent content noun (~23 per 10,000 words), which is
  worth knowing when checking whether a passage sounds right.
- **Übereinstimmung.** *Agreement / accord / correspondence / consonance* — one German word doing work
  English splits (§§241–242). *Effect:* when this skill says "agreement", hear Übereinstimmung — a
  consonance of practice and judgement, not a meeting of opinions.
- **Lebensform.** *Form of life* (§23, §241, PPF §345). *Effect:* the terminus of justification is the
  Lebensform — the shared, trained, practised way of living the language belongs to. Not a theory; a
  background. Note the discipline: the phrase occurs a handful of times in 330,000 words of corpus.
  Use it at that rate.
- **Sprachspiel.** *Language-game* — the whole "consisting of language and the actions into which it is
  woven" (§7). *Effect:* a language-game is an *activity*, never just a vocabulary.
- **Übersichtliche Darstellung.** *Surveyable / perspicuous representation* (§122). *Effect:* names the
  positive product of the method. When a Mode 2 answer needs a deliverable, this is it.

## The voice of this book

The dialectic is fuller here than anywhere else in the corpus, and its shape is consistent: a scene or
a case; a quoted temptation given its strongest form; a dash; the counter-move; often a question left
standing. One sentence in seven runs past forty words — the highest rate in the corpus — but the
median is nineteen, so the long sentence is a deliberate build, not the default. The *Randbemerkungen*
(the boxed marginal remarks in this edition) model the aside that undercuts a paragraph without
disrupting it.

## How this cluster changes the base skill
PI is where the skill lives, but four things were under-used before this build and are first-class
here now: the *picture held captive* (core move 2); *family resemblance together with its
counterweight* at §§71 and 88; *rule-following as practice* with the §211 and §217 bedrock passages;
and *surveyable representation* as the method's actual deliverable. Load this file for any rule-,
concept-, or picture-driven problem, for the German semantic range of the key terms, and whenever a
response needs the full dialectical shape rather than a verdict.
