# Method inventory — Remarks on the Philosophy of Psychology (Vol. I)

Register: investigative, first-person-dominant, imagined interlocutor. Measured: median sentence
~16 words, hedge:booster ≈ 2.4 (more tentative than RFM, less than C&V). This is the source for
aspect-seeing, the grammar of the inner, and psychological concepts as networks. Section numbers are
from Anscombe's edition of TS 229 (RPP I).

## Moves not already in the base skill

### 1. Aspect-switch ("seeing-as" ≠ seeing + interpreting)
- **Question it lets you ask:** Is this a dispute about the facts, or about which *aspect* of the
  same thing each party is taking? What does one aspect reveal that the other hides?
- **From the book:** the duck-rabbit figure — the same drawing, now seen as one animal, now the
  other; what is the beak under one aspect is the ears under the other (§§478 ff., §§508 ff.). The
  aspect **dawns** ("something strikes me!", §861 area); it is not neutral seeing with an
  interpretation added.
- **Serves:** Mode 2 (Engine A, generating alternative framings); Mode 1 (Sharpening 1, "What Is
  Being Said").

### 2. Continuous seeing vs. the dawning of an aspect
- **Question:** Is the person *reporting a steady perception* or *registering a change in how it
  strikes them*? These are two uses of "see," and confusing them breeds pseudo-problems.
- **From the book:** "continuous seeing as →" corresponds to a description that stays right without
  variation; the dawning is the description *changing* (§§863–864). Aspect-blindness is imagined as
  its own condition — some could not take the figure the second way at all.
- **Serves:** Mode 1 (distinguishing report from expression); Mode 2 (naming aspect-blindness in a
  stuck dispute).

### 3. The genealogical tree of psychological concepts (concept-network, not one essence)
- **Question:** Instead of "what *is* belief / expectation / intention?", ask how this concept sits
  among its neighbours — what it borrows, what it's contrasted with, where the family branches.
- **From the book:** "I should like to speak of a genealogical tree of psychological concepts," with
  a suggested likeness to the tree of number-concepts (§722). Anxiety "borrows the pictures of fear"
  (§724). No single core; a related family.
- **Serves:** Mode 2 (Engine B, family resemblance for forced either/ors); Mode 1 (resisting the
  demand for one essence behind a psychological word).

### 4. The grammar of the inner is fixed by its surroundings (expecting, intending, believing)
- **Question:** What in the *setting* — the circumstances, what came before, what the person goes on
  to do — makes this the expecting/intending/meaning it is? Not: what private episode accompanies it?
- **From the book:** across RPP the inner states are handled through their embedding, not through an
  introspected object; the expectation and its fulfilment are cut to fit each other in the practice,
  not linked by an inner mechanism. (Pairs with PI §337: "An intention is embedded in a setting, in
  human customs"; PI §580: "An 'inner process' stands in need of outward criteria.")
- **Serves:** Mode 1 (the "no inner object where grammar calls for a criterion" refusal); Mode 2
  (when a team posits a hidden "intent"/"understanding"/"preference" no procedure could check).

### 5. "The content of consciousness" is often a wrongly-framed question
- **Question:** Is the demand for the *content* of a state (what is the content of anxiety? of
  longing?) a real question, or a picture ("a mental image of longing") pretending to be one?
- **From the book:** "But what is the content… in anxiety? The question is wrongly framed" (§725);
  one thinks one has done everything by saying "a picture" (§726).
- **Serves:** Mode 1 (explanatory-mirage detection); Mode 3 (translating "content of experience"
  talk back into what a person notices, says, and does).

## How this cluster changes the base skill
The base skill treats the inner mostly as "avowal vs. report" (its pain example). RPP adds two
things: (a) *aspect* as a third category between fact and interpretation, which the inspector needs
for figurative and framing statements; and (b) *concept-networks*, which give Mode 2 its
family-resemblance engine for psychological and evaluative terms. Load this file whenever the
problem involves perception, framing, or mental-state vocabulary.
