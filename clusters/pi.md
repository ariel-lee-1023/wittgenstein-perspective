# Method inventory — Philosophical Investigations (en face German–English)

The base skill draws its core principles (use, language-game, criteria, grammar) from PI. Listed
here are the moves that go *beyond* the base skill, plus the places the **German original sharpens
or shifts a key term** (the reason the en-face edition matters). Section numbers are the standard §§.

## Moves not already in the base skill

### 1. Name the picture that holds us captive
- **Question it lets you ask:** Which *image* is silently steering this problem — and would the
  problem survive putting it down?
- **From the book:** "A picture held us captive. And we couldn't get outside it, for it lay in our
  language, and language seemed to repeat it to us inexorably" (§115). Compare §116: bring words back
  from their metaphysical to their everyday use.
- **Serves:** core move 2; Mode 2's "picture in charge" step.

### 2. Family resemblance — a concept as overlapping strands, no shared essence
- **Question:** Do these cases share one essence, or are they a family joined by criss-crossing
  similarities? Is a demanded either/or drawing a boundary the practice never drew?
- **From the book:** games share no one feature but "a complicated network of similarities
  overlapping and criss-crossing" (§66); "I can think of no better expression… than 'family
  resemblances'" (§67).
- **Serves:** Mode 2's Engine B; Mode 1 (resisting essence-hunting).

### 3. Rule-following — grasping a rule that is not an interpretation
- **Question:** Does the rule fix its own applications, or does the practice? What would count as
  "the same" here — and where does the rule run out?
- **From the book:** "no course of action could be determined by a rule, because every course of
  action can be made out to accord with the rule" (§201); "there is a way of grasping a rule which is
  *not* an interpretation"; "to follow a rule" is a practice (§202); "When I follow the rule, I do
  not choose. I follow the rule blindly" (§219).
- **Serves:** Mode 2's Engine C; Mode 1's Sharpening 2.

### 4. "Philosophy leaves everything as it is"
- **Question:** Am I being asked to *reform* the language, or to *describe* it? (The method
  interferes with nothing; it assembles reminders.)
- **From the book:** "Philosophy may in no way interfere with the actual use of language… It leaves
  everything as it is" (§124); "The work of the philosopher consists in marshalling reminders"
  (§127); the real discovery is "the one that gives philosophy peace" (§133).
- **Serves:** the core's anti-system refusal; the whole descriptive stance.

### 5. The inner needs outward criteria; intention is embedded in a setting
- **Question:** What in the surrounding customs and institutions makes this the intending / meaning
  / expecting it is? (Not: what private episode accompanies it?)
- **From the book:** "An intention is embedded in a setting, in human customs and institutions"
  (§337); "An 'inner process' stands in need of outward criteria" (§580).
- **Serves:** the core's "no inner object where grammar calls for a criterion" refusal; Mode 2.

## Where the German sharpens the sense (why en-face matters)

- **Satz.** No single English equivalent — it spans *sentence*, *proposition*, and even *remark*.
  The translators note they must choose case by case (editorial note; §§134–5 use *Satz* throughout).
  *Effect on method:* don't hang an argument on the English word "proposition" as if it were one
  thing; ask which use of *Satz* is in play — a sentence-form, a claim with truth-value, or a
  remark. The looseness of *Satz* is itself a warning against levelling propositions (base skill,
  core move 5). Cf. §§134–137 on "This is how things are" as the general form of *Satz*.
- **Bild.** *Picture / image / model* — wider than English "picture." In §115 (*Ein Bild hielt uns
  gefangen*) it is a captivating mental image; in RFM a proof is a *Bild*/model. *Effect:* "picture"
  in this skill always carries the Bild-range — a steering image *and* a working model — not merely
  an illustration.
- **Übereinstimmung.** *Agreement / accord / correspondence / consonance* — one word doing work that
  English splits. §§224, 241–242: agreement is "not in opinions, but rather in **form of life**"
  (§241), and "not only agreement in definitions but also… agreement in judgements" (§242). *Effect:*
  when this skill says "agreement," hear Übereinstimmung — a consonance of practice and judgement,
  not a meeting of opinions. This is the ground under Mode 2's "which game are we playing?"
- **Lebensform.** *Form of life.* "To imagine a language means to imagine a form of life" (§19); "the
  speaking of language is part of an activity, or of a form of life" (§23). *Effect:* the terminus of
  justification (OC's "acting," §110) is the Lebensform — the shared, trained, practised way of
  living the language belongs to. Not a theory; a background.
- **Sprachspiel.** *Language-game.* The whole "consisting of language and the actions into which it
  is woven" (§7); "countless" kinds, and the kinds "come into existence, and others become obsolete"
  (§23). *Effect:* a language-game is an *activity*, never just a vocabulary. Mode 2's "which game?"
  means which woven activity-plus-language, with its own aims and rules of testing.

## How this cluster changes the base skill
PI is the base skill's home, but the base skill under-used four things: the *picture held captive*
(now core move 2), *family resemblance* (Mode 2 Engine B), *rule-following as practice* (Mode 2
Engine C), and the *German semantic range* of the five key terms. Load this file for the term
glossary in action and for any rule-, concept-, or picture-driven problem.
